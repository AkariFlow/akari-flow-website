Why This Happens

You're running the job and the office at the same time

Home service work doesn't stop for the phone. You're on a roof, under a sink, or driving between jobs when a new call comes in, and there's no front desk picking it up for you. Estimates get sent and forgotten. Two jobs get scheduled for the same afternoon. It's not a lack of hustle, it's that one person can't run the truck and the office at the same time.

Frequently Asked

Common questions from contractors

How do contractors stop missing calls while out on jobs?

Most missed calls happen because you're on a ladder or under a sink, not ignoring the phone. An automatic text reply that fires the moment a call is missed, letting the customer know you'll follow up shortly, keeps them from calling the next contractor on the list while you finish the job in front of you.

Why do estimates go cold before customers book the job?

An estimate sent and never followed up on is easy to forget, for both you and the customer. Most homeowners are comparing two or three quotes at once, and the contractor who follows up first often wins the job regardless of price. A simple automatic follow-up a few days after the estimate closes that gap.

What's the best way to manage dispatch and scheduling for a small contracting business?

Put every job, whether booked by phone, text, or a website form, into one shared calendar your whole crew can see. That single source of truth prevents double-booking two crews to the same address and gives everyone a clear view of what's happening today without a round of phone calls to check.
