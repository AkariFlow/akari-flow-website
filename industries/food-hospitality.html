Why This Happens

Service doesn't stop long enough to catch up on the rest

Between service, prep, and everything in between, there's rarely a quiet moment to respond to a review or double-check tomorrow's reservation list. Staff updates get relayed shift to shift by word of mouth, which means something always gets lost in the handoff. It's not a lack of care, it's that the floor never stops moving long enough to get ahead of it.

Frequently Asked

Common questions from restaurants and hospitality businesses

How do restaurants reduce no-show reservations?

Most no-shows come down to a guest simply forgetting. An automatic confirmation when the reservation is made, followed by a reminder the day of, catches most of them before they become an empty table. Making it easy to cancel or reschedule with one tap also helps, since guests who can't easily change plans are more likely to just not show.

How should a small restaurant respond to online reviews?

Speed and consistency matter more than a perfectly worded response. A restaurant that responds to reviews within a day or two, good or bad, signals to future guests that someone's paying attention. The biggest risk isn't a bad review, it's a bad review that sits unanswered for weeks.

What's the best way to communicate with restaurant staff across shifts?

Scattering updates across texts, sticky notes, and word of mouth means something always gets missed between shifts. One shared place for schedules, shift notes, and updates, that every staff member checks, closes that gap without adding a new habit for the whole team to learn.
